How to read a loan repayment schedule

An amortization schedule is a table of every payment on your loan. Once you know how to read it, your loan statement stops being mysterious.

Reviewed 15 November 2026 · 5 min read

A small example

Consider a ₹10,00,000 loan at 9% for 5 years. The EMI is ₹20,758. The first six rows of the schedule look like this:

MonthEMIInterestPrincipalBalance left
1₹20,758₹7,500₹13,258₹9,86,742
2₹20,758₹7,401₹13,358₹9,73,384
3₹20,758₹7,300₹13,458₹9,59,926
4₹20,758₹7,199₹13,559₹9,46,367
5₹20,758₹7,098₹13,661₹9,32,706
6₹20,758₹6,995₹13,763₹9,18,943

What each column means

  • Interest. The opening balance times the monthly rate (9% ÷ 12 = 0.75%). In month 1 that is ₹10,00,000 × 0.0075 = ₹7,500.
  • Principal. What is left of the EMI after interest is paid, which actually reduces what you owe: ₹20,758 − ₹7,500 = ₹13,258.
  • Balance. The opening balance minus the principal portion. The next month’s interest is charged on this smaller figure, which is why the interest column falls and the principal column rises while the EMI stays the same.

The patterns to look for

  1. Interest falls every month, principal rises. Their sum is the constant EMI.
  2. The crossover. At some point the principal part overtakes the interest part. On long loans this happens late, sometimes more than ten years in.
  3. The balance ends at zero. If it does not, there is a rounding adjustment in the final instalment, or your rate changed.
  4. Total interest. Add the interest column to see the real cost of borrowing. For this loan it is ₹2,45,501.

Check your own loan against it

  • Enter your loan amount, rate and tenure in the EMI calculator, then compare the EMI and first-month interest with your statement.
  • If the EMI matches but the interest differs slightly, your lender may be computing interest daily or from specific dates.
  • If the numbers differ a lot, check whether the rate has been reset, whether fees were capitalised into the loan, or whether you have a pre-EMI period.
  • After a part-prepayment, confirm whether your lender shortened the tenure or lowered the EMI. The choice changes the saving.

The calculator can show the schedule by year or by month and download it as a CSV file, so you can keep a copy or open it in a spreadsheet.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my loan balance fall so slowly at first?

Because interest is charged on the full outstanding balance, so most of the early EMIs goes to interest, and only a small part reduces the principal.

What is amortization?

It is the process of paying off a loan through regular instalments, each covering interest and part of the principal, so that the balance reaches zero by the end of the term.

Will my bank&rsquo;s schedule match this exactly?

It should match closely for a standard reducing-balance loan. Small differences come from day-count conventions, rounding and any fee or rate changes.

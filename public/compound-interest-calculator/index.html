What compound interest means

Compound interest is interest earned on your original money and on the interest it has already earned. With simple interest, ₹1,00,000 at 8% earns the same ₹8,000 every year. With compound interest, the second year’s interest is calculated on ₹1,08,000, the third year’s on a larger amount still, and so on. The difference looks small at first and then becomes very large, which is why long horizons are so powerful.

The compound interest formula

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t P is the starting amount. r is the annual interest rate as a decimal (8% = 0.08). n is how many times a year interest is added. t is the time in years. A is the final amount, and A − P is the interest earned.

When you also add a deposit every month, this calculator converts your chosen compounding frequency into an equivalent monthly rate so that deposits and compounding line up, then adds each deposit at the end of its month. The result for a lump sum alone matches the formula above exactly.

A worked example: ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years

With interest added once a year, ₹1,00,000 grows to ₹2,15,892 after 10 years, so the interest earned is ₹1,15,892. Simple interest on the same amount would give just ₹80,000. The ₹35,892 gap is the extra that compounding adds.

The rule of 72

A quick way to estimate how long money takes to double is to divide 72 by the annual rate. At 8%, that is about 9 years, and the exact figure is 9.01 years. Read our guide to the rule of 72 for when it works and where it breaks down.

Where compound interest shows up

  • Savings and deposits. Bank fixed deposits usually add interest quarterly. See the FD calculator.
  • Investments. Reinvested dividends and growth compound over time. Use the SIP calculator for regular investing.
  • Debt. Credit card balances and some loans compound against you. Check the EMI calculator to see the true cost of a loan.
Taxes and inflation are not included. Interest is usually taxable, and prices rise over time, so your real gain is smaller than the headline figure. Treat results as a planning estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount. Compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus the interest already added. Over long periods, compound interest produces much larger sums.

Does daily compounding make a big difference?

Not usually. For ₹1,00,000 at 8% over 10 years, yearly compounding gives ₹2,15,892 and daily compounding gives ₹2,22,535. The rate and the time matter far more than how often interest is added.

What is the effective annual rate?

It is the yearly growth rate after compounding is taken into account. A nominal 8% compounded quarterly is an effective rate of about 8.24%. It lets you compare products that compound at different frequencies.

How do I calculate how long it takes to double my money?

Divide 72 by the annual rate for a quick estimate, or use the exact formula: ln(2) ÷ ln(1 + effective rate). The calculator shows the exact figure for a lump sum.

How are monthly deposits treated?

Each deposit is added at the end of its month and then earns interest from the following month. The compounding frequency you choose is converted to an equivalent monthly rate so that deposits and interest are consistent.

Is the result guaranteed?

The arithmetic is exact for the inputs you enter, but a real product may pay a variable rate, charge fees or tax the interest. Use the result to compare options, and check the terms of the actual product.

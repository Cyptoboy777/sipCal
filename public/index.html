How these calculators work

Every calculator here uses the standard formula for its job, shown in full on its own page, and then builds a year-by-year or month-by-month table so you can see where each rupee goes. We do not use hidden multipliers or rounded shortcuts. The loan calculator follows reducing-balance interest, the way banks quote most loans in India. The SIP, compound interest and fixed deposit calculators use compound growth, and each tells you which compounding it assumed.

Before publishing, we cross-check each engine against independent closed-form calculations: for example, that a loan schedule ends at exactly zero, that principal repaid adds back to the amount borrowed, and that a SIP matches the textbook future-value formula to the paisa. The worked examples in our articles are generated by the same code that powers the calculators, so the text and the tools cannot disagree.

What the calculators can and cannot tell you

A calculator is a way to see arithmetic, not a way to see the future. The EMI figure is exact for the rate and tenure you enter, but your real loan may have a floating rate, fees or insurance. A SIP result depends entirely on the return you assume, and real markets do not deliver a smooth line. A deposit calculation will match your bank’s certificate closely, but day-count rules can shift the final rupees. We label each result as an estimate and explain what is left out so you can plan with a clear head.

Four ideas worth understanding first

1. Early EMIs are mostly interest

On a ₹50.00 L loan at 8.5% for 20 years, 82% of the first instalment is interest. That is why a small prepayment in the first few years saves more than a large one near the end. See the prepay or invest guide.

2. Tenure is a trade between monthly comfort and total cost

A longer loan lowers the EMI and raises the total interest. The right tenure is the shortest one you can pay comfortably, with room for a bad month. The tenure guide has numbers.

3. Time matters more than the amount

For investments, the number of years you stay invested usually moves the result more than the instalment size. A ₹10,000 monthly SIP at a steady 12% grows to ₹50.46 L in 15 years, but the second half of that journey contributes far more than the first. Try different periods in the SIP calculator.

4. Small rate differences compound

One percentage point on a long loan is worth lakhs; one percentage point on a long investment is, too. Use the calculators to compare offers on equal terms rather than comparing headline rates alone. The rule of 72 is a handy mental shortcut.

Frequently asked questions

Are these calculators free?

Yes. They are free to use, with no sign-up. The site is supported by advertising.

Do you store the numbers I enter?

No. Calculations happen in your browser, and the figures you type are not sent to our servers.

Are the results exact?

The arithmetic is exact for the inputs you give, and each calculator states its assumptions. Real products can differ because of fees, taxes, floating rates and bank-specific rounding, so treat results as planning estimates.

Is this financial advice?

Because that is how most people in India think about money. ₹12,50,000 is shown as ₹12.50 L, and ₹1,25,00,000 as ₹1.25 Cr, with exact figures in the tables.
